[Introduction - مقدمة]
In ancient Greek philosophy, Thales asserted that the primary principle of all things was water. His theory was based on his observation of the moisture present everywhere in the world and was consistent with his view that the Earth floated on water.
Anaximander, a disciple and successor of Thales, proposed a different theory: earth, fire, air and water are unconnected substances. Instead, he proposed the existence of a phenomenon (an indefinite substance) from which all things arise and to which all things will return.
Anaximenes, a disciple of Anaximander, put forward yet another theory. He returned to the theory of the elements. Anaximenes suggested that everything is composed of air.
Heraclitus maintained that everything is in a state of flux. In such a system, matter is neither necessary nor possible.
Leucippus asserted that there existed indivisible particles, atoms, which underpin existence.

[I.1. State of the Matter - حالات المادة]
Matter consists of everything that possesses mass and occupies a volume in space. Matter exists in three states:
*   **Solid state:** These are rigid bodies that maintain a well-defined volume and shape; they are incompressible.
*   **Liquid state:** Liquids constitute a fluid state. They are deformable and take on the shape of the container that holds them.
*   **Gaseous state:** Gases occupy all the space available to them; they are compressible and expand easily.

[I.2. Change of State of Matter - تغيرات حالة المادة]
Heat and cold are essential factors in the change of state of matter. The different changes of state are represented in the diagram below. Note the use of the term liquefaction for a gas and condensation for a liquid-to-gas change of state.
*   Solid ⇌ Liquid: Fusion (انصهار) / Solidification (تصلب)
*   Liquid ⇌ Gas: Vaporisation (تبخر) / Liquefaction (تسيل)
*   Solid ⇌ Gas: Sublimation (تسامي) / Condensation (تكاثف)

[I.3. Concepts of Atoms and Molecules - مفاهيم الذرات والجزيئات]
**I.3.1 Atoms:**
Matter is made up of elementary particles called atoms. An atom is considered the smallest particle of an element and, according to its Greek etymology, is indivisible and indestructible.
There are 118 types of atoms; each element differs from the others in its structure, mass, and physicochemical properties. Each element is designated by an abbreviation or a symbol:
*   (رمز العنصر) ᴬ_Z X
*   Where A is the mass number and Z is the proton number.
*   The atom is an infinitesimally small quantity of matter whose mass is approximately 10⁻²⁶ kg and whose size is a few angstroms (Å). (1 Å = 10⁻¹⁰ m).
At our scale, and given this minuscule size, another quantity has been defined called Avogadro's number N_A (the kinetic theory of gases).

[I.4. Avogadro's number N_A - عدد أفوجادرو]
Number expresses the number of atoms present in 12 grams of carbon (12) or ¹²₆C. This constant has been measured by several very diverse and concordant experimental methods.
N_A = 6.032 × 10²³

[I.5. Molecules - الجزيئات]
A molecule is the association of two (O₂) or more atoms such as: NaCl, H₂O, N₂, O₂...

[I.6. Mole (unit of amount of substance) - المول]
The mole is the amount of substance of a system containing N identical entities. It is also defined as the amount of substance contained in 12 grams of carbon.
Therefore: 1 mole of atoms corresponds to N_A = 6.032 × 10²³

[I.7. Molecular molar mass - الكتلة المولية الجزيئية]
This is the mass of one mole of a molecule. It is equal to the sum of the molar masses of the atoms that constitute the molecule.
*Example (The mass of one mole of Zn(OH)₂ is):*
M(Zn(OH)₂) = M(Zn) + 2.M(O) + 2.M(H) = 65.37 + 2.(16) + 2.(1) = 99.37 g/mol
*The mass of molecule of Zn(OH)₂:*
99.37 g ---------> 6.023×10²³ molecules
m(Zn(OH)₂) -----> 1 molecule
m(Zn(OH)₂) = (1 × 99.37) / (6.023×10²³) = 1.649 × 10⁻²² g

[I.8. Molar volume - الحجم المولي]
This is the volume occupied by one mole of a substance. In the case of gases, and under standard conditions of pressure and temperature:
(n = 1 mol; T = 0°C; P = 1 atm): V = 22.4 L
*Example:*
5.38 × 10¹⁶ alpha particules He²⁺ lead to 2cm³ of helium gas under standard temperature and pressure conditions. Determine Avogadro's number.
2 × 10⁻³ L ---------> 5.38 × 10¹⁶
1 mol of He²⁺ -----> 22.4 L ---------> ? Atoms
N = (5.38 × 10¹⁶ × 22.4) / (2 × 10⁻³) = 6.025 × 10²³

[I.10. Atomic mass unit (amu) - وحدة الكتلة الذرية]
The atomic mass unit is defined as the fraction of the mass of a carbon atom 12.
1 amu = (1/12) × (12 / (6.023×10²³)) = 1.666 × 10⁻²⁴ g
1 amu = 1.666 × 10⁻²⁷ kg

*Example:* Determine the mass of a sodium (Na) atom knowing that its atomic molar mass is 23 g.
N atoms of Na (6.023×10²³ atoms) -------> 23 g
1 atom ---------------------------------> ?
m = (1 × 23) / (6.023×10²³) = 3.8 × 10⁻²³ g

[II.1. QUALITATIVE ASPECT OF THE MATTER - التصنيف النوعي للمادة]
(Classification of the material): Matter is made up of atoms that can be identical or different. From this, we can distinguish several types (See the flowchart below):
*   **MATTER (المادة)**
    *   **Pure substances (مواد نقية)**
        *   Pure substances Simple (عنصر بسيط) -> e.g., H₂, Fe, S
        *   Pure substances Compounds (مركب) -> e.g., H₂O, H₂SO₄
    *   **Mixtures (مخاليط)**
        *   Homogeneous (متجانس)
        *   Heterogeneous (غير متجانس)

**II.1. Pure simple substances:** They are made up of molecules whose atoms are identical (H₂, Fe, S, ...).
**II.2. Pure substances and compounds:** These are substances made up of molecules whose atoms are different (H₂O, H₂SO₄, ...).
**1. Mixtures:** A mixture is a substance composed of different molecules (NaCl + H₂O), (H₂O + oil).
We can distinguish:
*   **1/ HETEROGENEOUS SYSTEM (mixture) - نظام غير متجانس:** (Diagram shows oil and water not mixing, then shaking, then separating again).
*   **2/ HOMOGENEOUS SYSTEM (mixture) - نظام متجانس:** (Diagram shows Water + Syrup mixing after Agitation to form a Red liquid).
*   **3/ SOLUTION (المحلول):** This is a homogeneous mixture of pure substances that do not react with each other. The major component (in large proportion) is called the solvent, and the dissolved substances are solutes.
    *   Water + salt
    *   Water: solvent, salt: solute.
    *   A solution is a mixture formed when one or more solutes dissolve in a solvent.

*   **المعطيات:** لدينا محلولان، نريد مزجهما.

    C = n / V  =>  n = C * V

    V₁ = (C₂ * V₂) / C₁ = (0.2 * 2) / 0.5 = 0.8 L

*   **خلط المحاليل (Mixing):**
    V₁ + V₂ = 2

    0.5 * V₁ + 0.1 * V₂ = 0.2 * 2
    نعوض V₂ = 2 - V₁:
    0.5 * V₁ + 0.1 * (2 - V₁) = 0.4
    0.5 * V₁ + 0.2 - 0.1 * V₁ = 0.4
    0.4 * V₁ = 0.2

[Ex 4: Ideal Gas Law - قانون الغازات المثالية]
*   **المعطيات:**
    V = 594 cm³ (gas)
    P = 760 mmHg
    T = 29°C
    m = 1.08 g
    Calculate the molar mass (M).
*   **القوانين:**
    PV = nRT
*   **التحويلات:**
    P = 1 atm = 760 mmHg
    1 cm³ = 10⁻³ dm³ = 1 L
    R = 0.082 L.atm / K.mol
    T = 29 + 273 = 302 K
*   **حساب عدد المولات (n):**
    n = (P * V) / (R * T)
    n = (1.0066 * 0.594) / (302 * 0.082) = **0.024 mol**
*   **حساب الكتلة المولية (M):**
    n = m / M  =>  M = m / n
    M = 1.08 / 0.024 = **45 g/mol**

[Ex 5: Avogadro's Number Calculations - عدد أفوجادرو]
*   **المعطى:** N_A = 6.023 × 10²³
*   **1) كتلة ذرة أكسجين واحدة:**
    16 g  --->  6.023 × 10²³ (ذرة)
    m     --->  1 (ذرة)
    m = 16 / (6.023 × 10²³) = **2.656 × 10⁻²³ g**
*   **2) عدد الذرات في 1g من الأكسجين:**
    16 g  --->  6.023 × 10²³
    1 g   --->  n (عدد الذرات)
    n = (6.023 × 10²³) / 16 = **3.76 × 10²²**
*   **3) كتلة جزيء أكسجين واحد (O₂):**
    32 g  --->  6.023 × 10²³
    m(O₂) --->  1
    m(O₂) = (2 * 16) / (6.023 × 10²³) = **5.31 × 10⁻²³ g**
*   **4) عدد الجزيئات في 1g من الأكسجين:**
    32 g  --->  6.023 × 10²³
    1 g   --->  Z
    Z = (1 * 6.023 × 10²³) / 32 = **3.76 × 10²² mol**
